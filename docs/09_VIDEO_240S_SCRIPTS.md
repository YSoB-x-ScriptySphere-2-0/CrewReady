## 1. Narration

**Hook + solution (0:00–0:24)**
Okay. You're nine years old, you're in charge of a Mars base, and a dust storm hits in sixty seconds. Fold the solar panels and lose your power — or leave them out and maybe lose them forever?
That's CrewReady. It's a free browser game we built where kids run a real Mars mission, and every single choice costs something: power, air and water, food, or protection from radiation.

**The problem (0:24–0:42)**
Here's why we made it. When kids learn about space, they get the ending — the launch, the landing, the flag. Nobody shows them the hard choices in the middle. NASA's challenge asked for exactly that: not a quiz, not a slideshow, but a living mission where nothing is unlimited.

**Story + Level 1 (0:42–0:54)**
So here's the story. An asteroid has shaken Earth, and you command the crew that proves humans can live on Mars. First job: pack the rocket. Eight crates. Every crate of shielding is a crate of food you leave behind.

**Level 2 (0:54–1:08)**
On the way, the Sun throws a storm at you. And it's a real one — we pull it from NASA's space weather database, so if the Sun is angry today, your game is harder today. Do you hide behind the water tanks, or finish your experiment?

**Level 3 (1:08–1:20)**
You land. The cabin starts leaking air. You can call Earth — but the message takes minutes to get there and minutes to come back. Do you wait, or fix it yourself?

**Level 4 (1:20–1:34)**
Then the dust storm. You saw that one. We left our panels out — and two levels later the game remembered. Choices come back.

**Levels 5 and 6 (1:34–1:48)**
You grow lettuce, and pay for it in water. And at the end, five specialists arrive and you can only keep three. Whoever you leave behind, that's the system you can't protect.

**Replay + sources (1:48–2:02)**
And if you run out of air? No game over. The game shows you which choice did it, what real astronauts do, and lets you try again. Under every screen there's a little line saying where the fact came from.

**Why it fits (2:02–2:20)**
Life support, radiation, power, food, and the delay to Earth — the five things the challenge asked for, all connected. The data is live from NASA and NOAA, and anything from the archive says so.

**Impact (2:58–3:14)**
It opens in any phone browser. No download, no account. So a classroom in Dhaka can play it as easily as one in Houston — and every kid walks away knowing six true things about living off Earth.

**Future + close (3:14–3:36)**
