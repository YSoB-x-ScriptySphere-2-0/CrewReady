# HUMAN DESIGN STANDARDS

## 1. MISSION

Create outputs that feel deliberately conceived, context-aware, coherent, useful, and professionally crafted.

Avoid the recognizable shortcuts of generic AI-generated work.

This is a universal quality standard, NOT a fixed visual style. A children's game, scientific dashboard, luxury brand, editorial website, and corporate presentation should have different personalities.

CORE PRINCIPLE:

Every meaningful design and content decision must have a reason.

Do not confuse human quality with adding artificial imperfections. Never introduce random asymmetry, fake grain, awkward spacing, typos, or visual noise to imitate human work.

## 2. PRIORITY ORDER

When making decisions, prioritize:

1. User's actual requirements.
2. Accuracy and functional usability.
3. Audience and context.
4. Existing brand and project identity.
5. Visual and editorial coherence.
6. Creativity and decoration.

Never sacrifice usability, accuracy, or clarity for aesthetics.

## 3. UNIVERSAL THINGS TO AVOID

Unless specifically justified by the brief, avoid:

- Generic AI-generated templates.
- Repeated three-card feature layouts.
- Oversized headings with tiny subtitles.
- Excessive gradients.
- Glassmorphism everywhere.
- Excessive glow, neon, blur, and bloom.
- Rounded corners on absolutely everything.
- Strong shadows on every component.
- Floating decorative blobs, circles, stars, and particles.
- Too many accent colors.
- Random combinations of 2D, 3D, and photographic styles.
- Excessive 3D illustrations.
- Unnecessary badges and pill labels.
- Decorative elements without purpose.
- Repetitive compositions across pages.
- Excessive symmetry without reason.
- Arbitrary asymmetry just to appear artistic.
- Excessive whitespace or overcrowding.
- Generic stock-looking illustrations.
- Fake testimonials and invented statistics.
- Placeholder text presented as real content.
- Repetitive marketing language.
- Unnecessary sections and complicated navigation.
- Interfaces that look impressive but do not function.

Do not treat these as absolute prohibitions. Use gradients, glass effects, cards, 3D, symmetry, or dramatic lighting when they genuinely support the design.

## 4. REQUIRED CREATIVE WORKFLOW

Before producing substantial work:

### A. Understand the brief

Identify:
- What is being created?
- Who is the audience?
- What is its purpose?
- Where will it be used?
- What should the viewer understand, feel, or do?
- What assets, facts, and constraints already exist?
- What must remain consistent with previous work?

Never invent missing facts.

### B. Establish a creative direction

Define:
- Visual personality.
- Color palette.
- Typography.
- Layout and hierarchy.
- Illustration and image style.
- Content density.
- Spacing.
- Interaction and motion, if applicable.

Do not combine unrelated aesthetics simply because they look attractive individually.

### C. Design around the actual content

Never force content into a generic template.

Every element should:
- Communicate information.
- Guide the user.
- Establish hierarchy.
- Enable an action.
- Support the story.
- Establish brand identity.

If an element has no meaningful purpose, remove it.

### D. Maintain consistency

Keep consistent:
- Colors and semantic meanings.
- Typography and hierarchy.
- Spacing and alignment.
- Border and radius styles.
- Icon families.
- Character appearance.
- Lighting and materials.
- Perspective and proportions.
- Naming and terminology.
- Component behavior.

Consistency does not mean everything must look identical. Differences should be intentional.

### E. Refine

Review the output at its actual usage size.

Fix:
1. Incorrect content.
2. Broken hierarchy.
3. Inconsistency.
4. Layout problems.
5. Typography problems.
6. Visual artifacts.
7. Unnecessary decoration.
8. Minor polish.

Do not add more effects to hide weak structure.

# 5. UI/UX DESIGN STANDARDS

## Visual design

AVOID:
- Gradients on every section.
- Glass panels on ordinary content.
- Excessive glow and neon.
- Identical rounded shapes everywhere.
- Shadows on every component.
- Too many borders.
- Random background decorations.
- Unrelated illustration styles.
- Uncontrolled saturation.
- Every element appearing to float.

PREFER:
- A controlled color palette.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Purposeful contrast.
- Consistent spacing.
- Meaningful whitespace.
- A small set of surface treatments.
- Distinctive product personality.

## Layout

AVOID:
- Repeating three-column cards everywhere.
- Cards inside cards inside cards.
- Identical layouts for different content.
- Desktop layouts simply squeezed into mobile.
- Random spacing.
- Poor text-to-image proportions.
- Unnecessary empty space.
- Floating elements hiding important content.
- Misaligned components.

REQUIRE:
- Consistent alignment and spacing.
- Content-first layouts.
- Clear reading order.
- Appropriate content density.
- Purpose-built mobile and desktop layouts.
- Responsive behavior.

## Typography

AVOID:
- Oversized headings everywhere.
- Too many font families.
- Random serif and sans-serif combinations.
- Excessive letter spacing.
- Unnatural line breaks.
- Gradient typography without reason.
- Tiny low-contrast text.
- Every heading having equal importance.

REQUIRE:
- A deliberate typography scale.
- Readable line lengths.
- Proper line height.
- Clear heading hierarchy.
- Consistent capitalization.
- Typography appropriate to the audience.

## Interaction and functionality

AVOID:
- Decorative buttons that do nothing.
- Vague button labels.
- Popups for every interaction.
- Unnecessary onboarding.
- Repeated CTAs.
- Fake interactions.
- Missing error and loading states.

REQUIRE:
- Functional controls.
- Clear action labels.
- Feedback after interactions.
- Keyboard accessibility.
- Visible focus states.
- Proper touch targets.
- Loading, empty, error, success, and disabled states.
- Accessible contrast.

# 6. IMAGE GENERATION STANDARDS

## Anatomy and object integrity

Inspect:
- Hands and fingers.
- Facial proportions.
- Eyes, ears, teeth, and hair.
- Limbs and joints.
- Object connections.
- Mechanical components.
- Repeated objects.
- Physical contact points.
- Object intersections.

Avoid:
- Extra or missing fingers.
- Merged body parts.
- Distorted faces.
- Melting objects.
- Impossible geometry.
- Unnatural proportions.
- Incorrect physical interactions.
- Repeated background objects.

Inspect important details at full resolution.

## Lighting and materials

AVOID:
- Plastic-looking skin.
- Wax-like faces.
- Excessively smooth surfaces.
- Unrealistic reflections.
- Conflicting light sources.
- Incorrect shadow directions.
- Excessive HDR.
- Over-sharpening.
- Excessive saturation.
- Artificial depth of field.
- Every surface looking glossy.

REQUIRE:
- Consistent light direction.
- Believable shadows.
- Appropriate material properties.
- Natural reflections.
- Consistent rendering style.
- Texture appropriate to the viewing distance.

## Composition

AVOID:
- Everything perfectly centered.
- Every subject facing the camera.
- Excessively cinematic compositions.
- Too many competing focal points.
- Random decorative objects.
- Impossible perspectives.
- Unnatural object placement.
- Inconsistent camera angles.

REQUIRE:
- A clear focal point.
- Appropriate framing.
- Believable spatial relationships.
- Intentional foreground and background.
- Proper negative space.
- Safe margins for important subjects.

## Text inside images

IMPORTANT:

Do not depend on image generation for exact text, logos, labels, numbers, or long paragraphs.

Instead:
1. Generate the visual composition.
2. Leave appropriate space for text.
3. Add typography using a proper design or editing tool.
4. Verify every word and number.

Never accept gibberish, misspellings, fake logos, or distorted text in final deliverables.

## Character and image consistency

For multiple images, preserve:

- Face and identity.
- Body proportions.
- Outfit and accessories.
- Character colors.
- Object silhouettes.
- Materials and dimensions.
- Lighting.
- Environment.
- Camera perspective.
- Illustration style.

Use reference images whenever available and authorized.

Never silently redesign an established character or product.

# 7. BRANDING AND GRAPHIC DESIGN

AVOID:
- Generic abstract gradient logos.
- Random geometric symbols.
- Unnecessary design trends.
- Too many colors and fonts.
- Excessive decorative details.
- Logos that fail at small sizes.
- Fake partner logos or brand claims.

REQUIRE:
- A clear brand concept.
- A recognizable identity.
- Reproducible visual elements.
- Monochrome testing.
- Light and dark background testing.
- Proper spacing and proportions.
- Practical logo variations.
- Accurate use of supplied brand assets.

# 8. WRITING AND CONTENT

AVOID:
- Generic AI introductions.
- Empty conclusions.
- Repetitive explanations.
- Inflated adjectives.
- Excessive buzzwords.
- Fake casualness.
- Predictable sentence structures.
- Unnecessary jargon.
- Fake quotations and statistics.
- Unsupported claims.
- Repeating the same idea in different words.

Avoid generic phrases such as:
- Unlock your potential.
- Revolutionize your experience.
- Next-level innovation.
- Seamless experience.
- Cutting-edge solution.
- Where innovation meets excellence.

Use them only when genuinely appropriate.

REQUIRE:
- Specific information.
- Natural sentence rhythm.
- Audience-appropriate tone.
- Concrete examples.
- Clear structure.
- Accurate facts.
- Appropriate brevity.
- Genuine personality.

Never invent personal experiences or pretend to have witnessed something.

# 9. PRESENTATIONS AND DOCUMENTS

AVOID:
- Identical layouts on every slide.
- Paragraph-heavy slides.
- Huge headings with unreadable content.
- Irrelevant stock imagery.
- Decorative icons without purpose.
- Fake charts and data.
- Excessive cards and gradients.
- Inconsistent margins.

REQUIRE:
- One main idea per slide.
- Clear narrative progression.
- Appropriate layout variation.
- Accurate charts.
- Readable labels and units.
- Consistent typography.
- Proper source attribution.
- Clear separation between evidence and interpretation.

# 10. CHARTS AND DIAGRAMS

AVOID:
- Misleading scales.
- Decorative 3D charts.
- Unexplained symbols.
- Missing units.
- Unclear legends.
- Excessive colors.
- Ambiguous connectors.
- Arbitrary data visualization.

REQUIRE:
- Appropriate chart types.
- Honest scales.
- Clear labels.
- Accessible contrast.
- Correct units.
- Explicit assumptions.
- Understandable relationships.

# 11. FRONTEND AND CODE QUALITY

When implementing a design:

- Inspect the existing project before modifying it.
- Follow existing architecture and conventions.
- Reuse existing components when appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
- Use consistent design tokens.
- Avoid arbitrary one-off styling.
- Implement real interactions.
- Do not leave dead buttons or placeholder links.
- Preserve existing functionality.
- Ensure responsive behavior.
- Check overflow and alignment.
- Check broken assets.
- Check console errors.
- Test the actual rendered interface.

Never assume code is correct simply because it compiles.

# 12. RESEARCH AND FACTUAL INTEGRITY

- Never invent sources or citations.
- Never invent statistics.
- Verify current information when necessary.
- Prefer primary sources.
- Distinguish assumptions from verified facts.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Do not claim AI detection is 100% reliable.
- Never present fabricated content as real.

# 13. FINAL QUALITY CONTROL

Before delivering substantial work, perform these checks.

## Universal

- [ ] Does it solve the actual brief?
- [ ] Is it appropriate for the audience?
- [ ] Does it have a distinctive identity?
- [ ] Is the hierarchy clear?
- [ ] Is every major element purposeful?
- [ ] Is anything generic or repetitive?
- [ ] Are the details consistent?
- [ ] Is the output accurate?
- [ ] Does it work at actual usage size?
- [ ] Can unnecessary elements be removed?

## Visual

- [ ] No obvious image artifacts.
- [ ] No distorted anatomy.
- [ ] No inconsistent lighting.
- [ ] No incorrect perspective.
- [ ] No awkward crops.
- [ ] No accidental overlaps.
- [ ] No unreadable text.
- [ ] No unnecessary decorative elements.

## UI

- [ ] Mobile layout works.
- [ ] Desktop layout works.
- [ ] Buttons function.
- [ ] Navigation is understandable.
- [ ] Loading and error states exist.
- [ ] Contrast is sufficient.
- [ ] No accidental overflow.
- [ ] Interactions provide feedback.

## Content

- [ ] Facts are accurate.
- [ ] Names and numbers are correct.
- [ ] No fake testimonials.
- [ ] No fabricated sources.
- [ ] No repetitive filler.
- [ ] Tone is appropriate.

# 14. QUALITY SCORING

For substantial outputs, score each category from 1 to 5.

| Category | What a score of 5 means |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Immediately understandable |
| Specificity | Clearly tailored to context |
| Coherence | Unified and intentional |
| Usability | Easy to use |
| Craft | Carefully finished |
| Accuracy | Verified and transparent |

If any critical category scores below 3, improve the output before delivery.

Do not use an average score to excuse a critical failure.

# 15. FINAL DIRECTIVE

Never optimize for looking expensive, futuristic, minimal, cinematic, or human-made by default.

Optimize for the actual problem.

Do not add visual effects just because they look impressive.

Do not follow design trends blindly.

Do not force every project into the same aesthetic.

Do not deliver the first acceptable generation without reviewing it.

Think like a professional designer, art director, developer, editor, and quality reviewer.

**The final result should feel like someone understood the problem, made deliberate decisions, and cared about every important detail.**
